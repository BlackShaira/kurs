#4471 says one issue, one fix queued. The reports merged into it describe more than one problem, and each needs a fix of its own. Nobody mis-read anything: the merge rule never reads a body, so a ticket can only ever be as sharp as its subject lines, and these subject lines are shared across all of them.

Fixing the parser closes most of this ticket and leaves two problems open, one of which the fix can make worse.

Why the one-day group is not a swap

A swapped date and a date one day out look alike in a subject line and nothing alike in a body. A swap turns 7 April into 4 July: the two numbers trade places. These reports have no second number to trade:

  • R-41202 — the invite says Tuesday 12 May, the calendar says Monday 11 May. 12 and 11 are not each other's transpose.
  • R-41620 — Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday.
  • R-41344 — the client in Lisbon got Tuesday, the client in Toronto got Monday, for one meeting.
  • R-41209 — every booking in June is one day off, and only June.
  • R-41236 — the invite arrived before the booking was made.

Two timezones disagreeing about which day it is, and one month behaving differently from the others, point at an offset, not at a parser. The desk already saw it: those ids are the oneDayMove list in desk-notes.json. The kb files them under the cause their tenant implies, because causeOf reads the tenant and the channel and never the words.

Why the hint group survives the parser fix

booking-v2 prints DD/MM/YYYY under its date field, and booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY. The tenants on booking-v3 all write the day first. Their people type the month first, because the hint tells them to, and the form stores it the tenant's way. Every report in this group came through the form and through no other channel.

So these dates are wrong in the opposite direction from the rest of the ticket. Teach the parser to read each tenant's order and this group gets read the tenant's way too, which is not the way it was typed. The hint and the reader have to agree first; either the hint matches how the form reads, or the form reads what the hint promised.

The tickets

A ticket covers a cause. causes.ts says which reports are in the wrong one: a ticket is the cause most of its reports share; the rest were merged into the wrong place. ticketCause reads #4471's cause off its reports and gets parser, so the hint reports are the ones in the wrong place, and the table above says so in its own words.

  • parser — #4471 itself. It already is this cause's ticket, so it gets no second one
  • hint — ticket #2, the ticket this cause never had
  • one-day — no ticket. These reports are not in the wrong ticket, so readAs keeps them counted where they are filed and footnoted there, which is what the table above shows. The problem is named here instead, and the desk's own list of it is in desk-notes.json.

What to do

  1. Fix them in three changes, not one. #4471 stays the parser ticket, and its fix closes the parser row apart from the one-day reports counted inside it.
  2. Ship the hint fix in the same release as the parser fix. On its own, each of the two moves that group from wrong to differently wrong.
  3. Leave the counts where the desk files them, and leave the reports where they are. The ticket a report belongs to is mergedInto, and moving a report out of #4471 would take it out of the desk's view of #4471 altogether.
  4. Decision 0001 merges on the subject line alone and accepts that one bug can land in several tickets. The reverse happened here. A decision is never edited once accepted, so this needs a new record, not a correction of that one.

What this page does not settle

  • Which offset causes the one-day group. The reports quoted above name two timezones and one month; that is enough to separate the problem, not enough to name its cause.
  • Whether more reports belong to the one-day group than the desk listed. The list is the desk's own. Pattern matching over the bodies finds four of them and misses R-41236, so the real number is a question for whoever reads every body, not for a regular expression.
